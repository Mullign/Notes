​Finally, how do we integrate interaction design with other models of software design and development? ​We'll talk about that next. ​Integrating interaction design activities and other lifecycle models from other disciplines needs very careful planning. ​There are relevant software engineering models that allow for the integration of the interaction design activities. ​They include Extreme Programming, or XP, by Beck and Andres in 2005, Crystal by Cockburn in 2005, ​Strum by Schraber and Biel in 2002, and Adaptive Software Development, ASD, by Heisman in 2000. ​Several software engineering lifecycle models have been considered already, and many of them integrate the interaction design activities in them. ​The Agile software development framework emphasizes the following bullets that make integration with the interaction design activities easier. 

​It stresses the importance of iteration. It champions early and regular feedback. ​It handles emergent requirements that come up as you design alternatives, create prototypes, and evaluate them. ​Then you go back to a new set of emerging requirements, and these emerging requirements might actually come up oftentimes. ​And it aims to strike a balance between the flexibility and structure overall of the system.
