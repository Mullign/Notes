​Okay, so let me share with you some more points about why we need to prototype. ​What's the need for prototyping? ​Prototyping is very central to the evaluation and feedback gathering in interaction design ​with regard to the design of the product and various stages during construction. ​Because a central tenet of interaction design is user-centric design, construction and evaluation. ​And not just from a software design perspective, thinking about software design from software ​design best principles, but also including user-centric aspects, rigorous user-centric ​and feedback with regard to the design, construction and evaluation, final evaluation, the summative evaluation of a product. ​So that's the central idea behind interaction design is that all of these activities has to be done in a user-centric fashion. ​So the prototypes allow stakeholders to be able to see and hold and interact with the prototype. 

​And it's more easy than perhaps trying to envision how the look and feel of a product might be from a digital drawing, ​or from perhaps paper drawings of a product. ​So the prototype allows for this kind of very multi-modal interaction, if you'd like, ​with the version of the product for stakeholders, as well as potential users of the system. ​The design team member can also communicate effectively about the nature of the product and perhaps the look and feel of the product ​and its perceptual and motor aspects. ​So the placement of buttons, the placement of text areas, the placement of the size and scale of the information displays, ​all of that can be communicated much more effectively when you have a prototype in place among team members. ​Then just if it is an idea that you're discussing, it's just something that one has to imagine. ​And you can test out ideas for yourself. You can also brainstorm when you have a prototype in place. 

​It gives you something tangible to think about, rather than just picturing it from your mind's eye ​and thinking about the pros and cons of the design from that perspective. ​So it encourages reflection. It's a very important aspect of design is to be able to introspectively think about the look and feel of the product, ​the form factor of the product, the functionality of the product, how it should be layered, et cetera, just ideas. ​And it's much more easier to do this when you have a prototype in place. ​And prototype answers questions and support designers to choose between alternatives to the design process, ​either for the stage that they're in or even future designs based on the success of a prototype.
