![[Introduction to Remote Conversation Slides.pdf]]With regards to remote conversations, much research has been done on how to support conversations when people are at a distance from each other. Many applications have been developed for this type of remote conversations that are somewhat asynchronous, and where users may or may not be present in a face-to-face setting, or they may not be present simultaneously as part of the dialogue. An email is a very good example of asynchronous conversation. Synchronous face-to-face conversation, examples are video conferencing, video phones, instant messaging, and chat rooms. Chat rooms, however, can basically facilitate synchronous face-to-face conversation, or asynchronous messaging as well, where people can leave messages, and then others can respond as and when they want, and they don't necessarily have to be there at the same time. Do they mimic or move beyond existing ways of conversing? It's an interesting question to ponder.
