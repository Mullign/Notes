![[Persuasive Technologies and Behavioral Change Slides.pdf]]Now let's talk about another class of technology. These are persuasive technologies and how they induce behavioral change. And the idea here is that for years, traditional media has been used to persuade people to join a good cause or to give up bad habits, to donate money and to do basically social good or charity or buy a product and to persuade people to join a political party, etc. So technology that are meant to persuade people to do things and to change their behavior or opinions are called persuasive technology. Interactive technologies have also been used to sort of entice, cajole and persuade people to do something that they may not have otherwise done. So the definition of persuasive technologies is essentially interactive computing systems that are deliberately designed to change people's attitudes and behaviors. And a diversity of technology has been created that are now used to change what users do or think. 

These include things like pop-up ads, warning messages, reminders and prompts, maybe even personalized messages for specific users, recommendations provided by recommender systems. And another successful example includes the Amazon one-click mechanism, making it easy or tempting to purchase a product. Recommender systems that suggest specific books, hotels, restaurants may also nudge the user towards buying a product. Safety, preventive health, health care, fitness, etc. Energy consumption, personal relationships and learning. These are all various types of products or potentially topics that can be persuaded by technology. And the emphasis is on changing habits or doing something that will improve an individual's well-being through monitoring their behavior. 

The Nintendo's Pocket Pikachu is a good example of a persuasive technology. So it actually induced changing bad habits and improving well-being, is designed to motivate children to be more physically active on a regular basis. The wearer receives credit with each step taken. So it's essentially a precursor to today's Fitbit, Jawbone, etc. The currency being watts that could be used to buy Pikachu presents in Pikachu's site. 20 steps on a pedometer rewarded the player with one watt that they can collect and use to buy Pikachu presents and Pikachu products. The use of positive rewarding and sulking can be a powerful means of persuasion, given that children often become emotionally attached to their virtual pets, especially when they start to care for them. So the owner of the digital pet that lives in the device is required to walk, run, or jump. 

If the owner does not exercise, the virtual pet becomes angry and refuses to play anymore. So how effective is the use of novel forms of interactive technologies? Example the combination of sensors and dynamically updating information that monitor, nag, or send personalized messages intermittently to a person more effectively to change a person's behavior than non-interactive methods, such as the placement of warning signs, labels, or ads in prominent positions. There are technologies such as Waterbots, a system that was developed using a special monitoring and feedback device for adults as a way of reducing their usage of water in their homes. And there is evidence that people are wasteful of water and leave their tap running when doing household chores or brushing their teeth. The research team thought that the use of monitoring technology could help persuade householders to change their behavior to be more conservative in their water usage. To this end, they devised a system of positive enforcement which states that the activities are likely to be repeated if some kind of reward is given occasionally and randomly, similar to the reward system used in slot machines. 

A sensor-based system was developed where positive auditory messages and chimes were sounded when the tap was turned off. The water was also lit with a random pattern of LED color as a reward for consistent water-saved behavior. And there is also use of peer pressure. Two illuminated bar graphs were also presented alongside the tap showing how much water a person had used relative to someone else in the household. The idea was to encourage peer pressure and for the members of the household to talk to each other about the water usage. And the usability studies that were done on this particular system suggest that this was most effective method of persuasion. So some of the examples of persuasive technologies is shown here, which is most effective. 

The photo on the left is of a banner placed in downtown LA in an attempt to encourage the general public to take stairs instead of the elevator, asking people to climb stairs on a certain day. Two photos on the right are ambient displays designed to do the same thing, but using more subtle and interactive methods. Lights that twinkled when people approached them intended to lure them to take the stairs and clouds of different colored spheres that move up and down depending on how many people have taken the stairs or the elevator for a given period of time. Gray represents elevator. So the higher the orange cloud is relative to the gray one, the more people are taking the stairs than the elevator. Which representation do you think is most effective? The banner on the left uses this striking slogan that metaphorically points a finger at you. 

So some people might find this amusing or a challenge. However, others may likely to find it offensive and even insulting and ignore it. So the extent to which it will change behavior is debatable. In contrast, the ambient displays shown in the other images are meant to be more subtle and aesthetic. They are assumed to work by raising people's awareness of a behavior that are normally overlooked or try not to think about it and to nudge them at a point of decision making. An in-the-wild study was conducted when they were embedded in a building showed a significant increase in people taking the stairs when they had these kinds of art-like looking persuasive technologies. Interestingly, the majority of people when asked were not aware of changing their behaviors in response to noticing these orange and gray balls. 

This suggests that ambient displays like the ones that are shown in the pictures can influence people at an unconscious level, perhaps at a visceral level, and they can be used to encourage people to adhere to certain kinds of desired behaviors where there is a choice such as washing hands or not, eating healthy or unhealthy snacks, taking one route or another through a building, switching off the lights or leaving them on.
