And as commented by one of Wufoo's creators, Kevin Hale, the inspiration for our color palette did come from our competitors. He wanted to make it more like Fisher Price's toy, for instance, in terms of the look and feel of the form itself. And in that way, he wanted to evoke sort of familiar feelings that one has with toys that is evoked by its color, its form, and its essentially font, for instance, and certain appearance or related aspects. And he wanted to design it in such a way that it evokes positive emotional reactions in users. And he said, it is really depressing to see so much software designed to remind people they're making databases in a windowless office. And so we immediately knew we wanted to go in the opposite direction. And he further said that the goal of the company was to feel like something Fisher Price, which is a toy maker, would actually make, rather than something that computing researchers would potentially create as a form with lots of details and that actually doesn't have a very appealing look and feel. 

So essentially, Wufoo's design policy is to make the experience of filling out these forms fun and enjoyable as possible.
