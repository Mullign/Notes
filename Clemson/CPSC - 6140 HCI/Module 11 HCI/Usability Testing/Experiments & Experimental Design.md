​Matched participant is where participants are matched in pairs based on perhaps their ​expertise or gender or ethnicity or what have you, and then they are perhaps put in ​different conditions. ​So each matched pair has the exact same demographics or characteristics, and then each member of ​that match is then assigned to one of the two conditions. ​This slide here shows the advantages and disadvantages of the three different methods of participant ​assignment to a research experiment that I showed in the previous slide. ​So when it comes to different participants, the advantage is that there are no order effects ​in the sense that each participant is only assigned to one of the conditions of the experiment. ​So there is no order effect in the sense that if a participant saw all the conditions of ​the experiment in a random order, one after the other, what the participant experienced ​in the previous condition will affect what the participant is doing potentially in the ​current condition, or may affect what the participant is doing in the current condition. ​This is called an order effect. ​So when you have different participants, the advantage is that there would be no order ​effect because they only experience one condition. 

​Disadvantage on the other hand is that you may require many subjects, and individual ​differences between the participants could be problematic. ​Now if you have the same participant who try all the different conditions in some random ​order, then you may need only fewer individuals to conduct the study. ​That's an advantage. ​And there is no individual differences per se from one participant to another, or from ​one participant in one condition to the same participant in another condition because it ​is the same individual. ​The disadvantage, however, is that you have to counterbalance because you want to make ​sure that the order in which the conditions that the participant sees, there is equal ​number of participants seeing condition A first and then condition B second, as there ​are participants that are seeing condition B first and then condition A second. ​So that needs to be counterbalanced. ​Now matched design, the advantage is that they are the same as different participants, ​so it's similar to having no order effects, that's the advantage here.
