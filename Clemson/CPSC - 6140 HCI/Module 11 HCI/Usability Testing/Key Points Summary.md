​So in this last slide, ​I want to point out some key points or lessons learned from this chapter. ​So the first point is that usability testing ​typically takes place in controlled usability labs or temporary labs. ​Usability testing focuses on performance measures, ​example, how long and how many errors are ​made when completing a set of predefined tasks or trials. ​Indirect observation could be videos and keystrokes that are logged by the system. ​User satisfaction questionnaires and interviews that are ​administered by the researchers at the end of the study are also collected. ​Affordable remote testing systems are more portable than usability labs. ​So we looked at the lab in a box type systems, ​and many also contain mobile eye tracking and ​other devices that might be application-specific. 

​Experiments, on the other hand, ​test a hypothesis by manipulating certain variables while keeping others constant. ​The experimenter controls independent variables, ​such as the conditions of the experiment, ​in order to measure dependent variables, ​which are the performance measures. ​Field studies are evaluation studies that are carried out in ​natural settings to discover how people interact with technology in the real world. ​Field studies that involve the deployment of prototypes or technology in ​natural settings may also be referred to as in-the-wild studies. ​Sometimes the findings from a field study are unexpected, ​especially for in-the-wild studies, ​in which the researchers explore how novel technologies are used by ​participants in their own homes, places of work, or outside, ​especially in context-specific settings. ​Thank you for your attention.
