​The usability testing involves recording performances of typical users of the system, ​performing typical tasks that the system is designed to facilitate. ​So they are usually done in a control setting, such as a usability lab, or perhaps a usability ​center, in which the participant or the potential user of the system can be carefully monitored ​by the designers and developers of the system. ​And they can be potentially monitored either via natural observation or through video camera. ​And they can be given very specific tasks that the users need to accomplish. ​And then in the process of the user performing that task, the user is observed either via ​video camera, audio devices, if they have to speak into a microphone or what have you, ​in a think-aloud protocol, or as they are interacting with the system using speech. ​And then the participant or user is timed with regard to how long it takes them to complete ​that task. ​Data is recorded on video, and key presses of the participant or user are also logged ​by the system, potentially automatically. 

​That data is then used to calculate performance times, how long it takes to complete the task ​or the trial, and to identify and explain errors later, once the study is complete. ​User satisfaction is evaluated using questionnaires that are given either intermittently or towards ​the end of the usability study. ​Oftentimes it's given at the end of the usability study. ​And then the designers or the researchers may also interview the participant or the ​user to get data and feedback with regard to the likes and dislikes of using the system ​and the user's impressions of the system. ​Field observations may be used to provide contextual understanding in the sense that ​field observations are different than typical usability testing in that it may not be done ​in the lab. ​So field observations are where the software is used by potential users of the system in ​the context of their workplace, so where they might naturally use this software. ​If it is used during driving, let's say, or walking to look for a restaurant or something ​like that.
