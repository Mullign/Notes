​Now, the data gathering guidelines are as follows. ​First, focus on identifying the stakeholders and their needs. ​Identify stakeholders may be achieved by studying behaviors and support tools, ​or by looking at other products, ​such as a competitor's products, ​or an early release of a product under development. ​So, involve all the stakeholder groups. ​It's very important to involve all the stakeholders in the group and its association. ​In one case with a very large distribution and logistics company, ​a system was developed by interviewing warehouse staff, ​clerics, and managerial people. ​But when the system went live, ​production dropped by 50%, ​because the bottleneck was that when developing the system, ​the warehouse supplier was not involved in the design and development of the system, ​and it was incompatible with theirs. 

​So, no one had asked the warehouse staff, for example, ​the warehouse supplier, ​how they work. ​And the new system was incompatible with the working routines ​in the warehouse side of things. ​So, involving only one representative from each stakeholder in the group is not enough, ​especially if the group is very large. ​So, everyone involved in the data gathering will have their own perspectives on the situation, ​the task, and their job, ​and how they interact with them. ​So, if you only involve one representative stakeholder, ​then you will only get a very narrow view. ​And so, it's important to use also a combination of data gathering techniques. ​And supporting the data gathering session with suitable props, ​such as task descriptions and prototypes, if available, ​is very important. 

​Since the requirements activity is iterative, ​prototypes or descriptions generated during one session ​may be reused and revisited in another ​with the same or different set of stakeholders. ​Using props may also help to jog people's memory ​and act as a focus for discussion ​among the group of representatives from the stakeholder groups.
