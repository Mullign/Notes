So optimization could mean, for instance, minimizing the number of errors, maximizing the time it takes in order to use that product, or a host of other quantitative and qualitative variables that need to be optimized between users and a product. So let's walk through some of these aspects. When it comes to understanding users need, you need to take into account what people are good and bad at, and what they may also like or dislike, for instance. And if it requires a lot of fine motor actions, perhaps most people are not going to be good at performing such actions. So you need to take into account users' capabilities and their skill sets, especially your target or intended users of the system. Consider what might help people in the way that they currently do things. So you could have hints, you could have helpful messages, you could have some tips that are provided by the system from time to time that might guide the user as they're using the system. 

And you can think through what might provide quality user experiences. So user experiences have to do with some emotional attributes that are evoked in users when they're using the system. So what sort of positive emotional attributes can you evoke as users are using the system so they can take a liking towards the system, and they will have propensity to use the system time and time again, and that could provide a pleasurable user experience for them. And listen to what people want and get them involved. One of the things that product designers must do often with systems is they should involve the users from an early stage. This is often called participatory design. So users are often involved in the design of the product, not only the final summative evaluation of the product, but in the design, in the prototyping phase. 

So as prototypes are introduced, you may do some focus group testing of those prototypes. And also to finally evaluate the end result, the end product with the actual intended users of the system are also very important. And use tried and tested user-centric methods. So when you involve users, such as the case of the participatory design that I mentioned, through the design, choosing alternatives, the prototyping, and the evaluation aspects of the system, it is called a user-centric method of designing, implementing, and evaluating the system. So oftentimes there are lots of best principles and practices, well-known practices, well-known evaluation techniques that involves user-centric methods. So oftentimes try to use these kinds of tried and tested user-centric methods during the design stage, the prototyping, and the evaluation stage. We will look at many of these tried and tested user-centric methods in later chapters.
