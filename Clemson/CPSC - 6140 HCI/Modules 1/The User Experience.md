![[The User Experience Slides.pdf]]The next topic is also the user experience. So what is the user experience? It essentially describes how the product behaves and is used by people in the real world. So this involves the way people feel about it and their pleasure and satisfaction or dissatisfaction when using it and looking at it, holding and opening and closing it. For instance, every product that is used by someone has a user experience. Take, for example, the newspaper, ketchup bottles, reclining armchairs, cardigans, sweaters, etc. And it involves things like iPhones, iPads, Android phones, every product that every computing system has some kind of user experience associated with it. You cannot design a particular user experience, but you can design for a user experience. And if you want users to have some kind of a pleasurable experience, or maybe users to have a satisfying experience using a game or a product or what have you, you can design for it, but you cannot design the user experience that actually comes out of it. So it's difficult to evoke those exact feelings that you want to evoke in users. It's oftentimes that users may, you know, generate a particular type of feeling towards it. You can assess whether that is a positive or negative feeling or whether it lines up with the intended emotions that you want to evoke in users. But you can't specifically design to evoke that specific emotion per se. That's quite hard to do. And so the user experience involves designing in such a way that you evoke positive emotional experiences with regards to using the product. Let's talk about a case in point here. The iPad Nano Touch was one that was designed in such a way that it involves a very simple button-based interaction. The icons were laid out in a very intuitive manner. The icons explicitly stated what that functionality would do in this iPod Nano. The iPod came with different color schema. So users could buy an iPod that's customized to their liking. So they might buy a color that is a color that they really like and they want to use. It's a very simple, elegant design. It's a very distinctive branding. And the user experience was found later on to be pleasurable. And it turned out even to be a must-have fashion item for a lot of teens and young adults. And it was catchy. It even had, users were able to give it a name. They were even able to etch their name and the name that they gave to this product in the back of the product. It was pretty cool. And it was an instant success in a way that perhaps even Apple did not design for and did not expect. And it resulted from a quality user experience from the start. So what Apple did was to design a product that was simple and elegant and create a distinctive branding aspect. And the user experience essentially came about through this. And they later discovered that the effective design that they came up with resulted in a positive user experience, one that evoked pleasurable user experiences, involved users wanting to buy this product as a fashion item, and one that users particularly liked and that resulted in high amount of satisfaction. What's involved in the process of interaction design? There are four key steps to interaction design. They involve the following. 

It's basically establishing requirements as the very first step. And there are specific chapters that are dedicated to each one of these steps in the interaction design process. And once you have established what the requirements are, you would look for the background of the users, what the task that is done between the system and the user, and what is required in order for the system to be effective and efficient. And you would think about those requirements, and you would then design multiple alternatives, multiple options for the system. And during this design phase, as well as the establishing requirements phase, it would not only involve multidisciplinary teams of researchers and developers and thinkers and philosophers, but also it would involve potential members of the end-user community. And then you would have various prototypes that would be engineered based on those design alternatives. Perhaps from 10 design alternatives, you would pick five and develop these prototypes. And it would involve evaluating these prototypes to see which one would be the one, and which particular prototype is the one that has the highest usability and user experience for the end user. Four characteristics of interaction design is that the user should be involved through the development of the project, through the development of the product by this company, as well as the project itself. Specific usability and user experience goals need to be identified, such as usability goals could involve efficiency, effectiveness, ease of use, learnability, etc. User experience goals could involve, for instance, that the product needs to be satisfying, the product needs to be friendly in terms of perception, the product needs to be likable, and so on. And these all need to be identified and clearly documented and agreed from the beginning of the product or for the project as the end goals or the objectives of the product. And iteration is needed through these core activities. So you could potentially start from establishing requirements to designing alternatives, prototyping, evaluation, and you could, you know, do one iteration after another, go through these four steps over and over again, through several iterations in order to then arrive at a most effective and best product for the end user.
