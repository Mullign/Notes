So we are at the end of the chapter. I want to leave you all with regards to the first chapter lecture with a few key points or take home messages. One is that interaction design is concerned with designing interactive products to support the way people communicate, interact in their everyday lives, and working lives with the product and the software systems. It is concerned with how to create quality user experiences. And it requires taking into account a number of independent factors, including the context of use the type of activities performed by users, cultural differences, interpersonal differences and user groups. It's the process that is certainly multidisciplinary, involves many input from a wide-reaching discipline such as psychology, social psychology, interpersonal communication, software engineering, etc. And many different fields as well, including human factors.
