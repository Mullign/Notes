Are they the general public? Are they a group of participants? Are they individuals? All of that is also important. The nature of the techniques can also matter. Sometimes a direct observation is necessary. Sometimes you want indirect observation where one may need to be a passive observer rather than an active observer. 

Resources available to conduct the study and the time available to conduct the study, all of them need to be taken into consideration. So finally, a summary of the topics that we have studied in this chapter include the following. So data gathering sessions should have clear goals. An informed consent process may be needed before you start gathering data from participants. Five key issues of data gathering are goals, choosing participants, triangulation, participant relationship, and piloting the study before the main study is conducted. Data may be recorded using handwritten notes, audio or video recordings, a camera, or any combination of these. And interviews may be structured, semi-structured, or unstructured.
