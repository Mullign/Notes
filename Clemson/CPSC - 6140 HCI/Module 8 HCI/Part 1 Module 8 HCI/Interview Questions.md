There are two types of interview questions. One is essentially closed questions that have a predetermined answer format. For instance, it can be a yes or no answer or a true or false type of answer. Open questions that do not have a predetermined format. So open questions are suitable when the goal of the session is exploratory. So you want to have as much participants' self-initiated responses as possible that you can gather in an open question. Closed questions can only be used where the possible answers may be known in advance, and so it's much more specific. 

So the guidelines for closed questions are that compound sentences can be confusing. So split them up into two separate questions if possible. Try to be as specific as possible. Avoid long questions. For example, how do you like the smartphone app compared with previous ones that you have owned, to, for instance, how do you like the smartphone app as the first question, and then followed by, have you owned other smartphone apps? And then if they say yes, if so, how do you like them? So you kind of stage these questions by breaking apart larger questions into smaller ones where you can record the answers step by step. 

Interviewees may not understand complex jargon or complex language that may be too embarrassing and may be too embarrassed to admit, so you need to take into account that the interview questions are simple for the general audience to understand, and it doesn't use complex technical jargon or things that could be confusing to the interviewee. Try to keep questions neutral. For example, why do you like the style of interaction? This question that the person does not like it and will discourage some interviewees from stating their real feelings. Instead, you can say, what are your impressions of the style of interaction? Or what do you like or dislike about the style of interaction?
