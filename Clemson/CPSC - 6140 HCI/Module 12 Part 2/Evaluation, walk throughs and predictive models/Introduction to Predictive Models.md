​Now let's look at predictive models. ​Predictive models essentially provide a way of ​evaluating products or designs without directly involving users. ​They involve typically mathematical modeling ​of the expected performance of users. ​That requires some usability testing, ​but it's essentially models the performance and so you can ​predict what users are going to do based on that model. ​Less expensive than user testing, ​and its usefulness is limited to systems with predictable tasks. ​Examples include telephone answering systems, ​mobiles, cell and smartphones. ​It's based on expert error-free behavior. 

​So a predictive model essentially ​evaluates a system without users being present, ​but the model itself may have been ​developed with feedback and studying ​how users interact with the system. ​So it's very data-driven, ​so it requires lots of a priori data with regards to how ​users have interacted with the system and ​the feedback that have been provided ​by users when they have interacted with the system. ​But once the predictive model is formulated and evaluated, ​they are generalizable resources that can be used to ​design the interactions for a new product. ​That uses the same aspects of ​the interaction of previous applications ​from which the data was gathered. ​Because of the generalizable method will work for any user, ​aspects of the model can involve expert evaluators, ​role-playing users in inspections. ​Predictive models are used as ​formulas to derive measures of various performance. ​So the predictive model provides estimates of the efficiency, ​that is time taken to complete the task, for example, ​of different systems for various kinds of tasks. 

​For example, a smartphone designer ​might choose to use a predictive model ​because it can enable her to determine ​accurately which is the optimal layout of keys on ​the phone for allowing ​common operations to be performed together. ​By looking at how much time it takes to select keys on ​a smartphone touchscreen interface through ​previous evaluation methods and predictive models that have been ​derived from a priori data of users use of such interfaces.
