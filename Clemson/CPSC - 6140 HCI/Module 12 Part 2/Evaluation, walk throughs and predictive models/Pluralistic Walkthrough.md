​Pluralistic walkthroughs are another type of walkthrough in which users, developers, ​and usability experts work together closely to step through a task scenario, discussing ​usability issues associated with those steps of the task, with the dialogue elements in ​the interface involving the scenarios and the steps of the scenarios and the process ​by which users are expected to accomplish tasks using that system and its interface. ​So it's a variation of the cognitive walkthrough theme, and it's performed by a carefully managed ​team that consists of users, developers, usability experts that walk through to step through ​a task scenario, discussing usability issues associated with dialogue elements involved ​in the scenario steps, in the mock user system interaction scenario steps. ​And the panel of experts begins by working separately, then each evaluator, participant, ​user, developer, and usability expert is asked to assume the role of a typical user. ​And the scenarios of use and a few prototypes screens are given to each evaluator who writes ​down the sequence of actions without consulting other analysts. ​And then finally, the panelists discuss the actions, comes to a consensus before moving ​on to the next round of the screens, and they repeat the same process, steps one, two, and ​then finally three, and comes to a consensus recommendation. ​When there is a managed discussion that leads to an agreed decision, the approach itself ​lends itself well to participatory design and also other adaptations of basic cognitive ​walkthroughs. ​So the benefits is that this level of analysis is invaluable for safety-critical systems. 

​However, one limitation is that having to get all the experts together at once is a ​scheduling problem, per se, in logistics. ​And then proceeding at the rate of the slowest expert in terms of pace of evaluating the ​product and collecting the expert's recommendation, only a limited number of scenarios can be ​examined due to time constraints.
