​Inspections involve using subject matter experts in the inspection of a software system and ​evaluate its usability. ​There are several different kinds. ​And the idea here is to understand users through knowledge codified in heuristics, or data ​collected remotely, or models that predict users performance. ​Note that none of these methods require users to be present during the evaluation, because ​we are using subject matter experts in the evaluation process to act as users. ​So experts use their knowledge of users and technology to review the software usability. ​That is, the inspection method typically involves an expert role-playing the user for whom the ​product is designed, analyzing aspects of the interface and identifying any potential ​usability problems by using a set of guidelines. ​Experts critique can be formal or informal. 

​And the most well known are heuristic evaluation and cognitive evaluation. ​So there are two different varieties. ​Heuristic evaluation is a review guided by a set of heuristics, or rules, or guidelines. ​Walkthroughs involve stepping through a pre-planned scenario, noting potential problems.
