An example of an input device is this example right here. So this was work that was done by one of my graduate students, Daval Parmar. And basically, what Daval Parmar did was he used the Razor Hydra, which is essentially a 3D spatial input device for both the left hand and right hand. So you can interact with objects using 3D movements of your left and right hands that are detected by the Razor Hydra for two-handed interaction. And then you also had two thumbsticks. So you can do some small scale type of movements of the virtual cursor in 3D using those thumbsticks on the dominant and the non-dominant hand. And using this, Daval actually studied how, for instance, the spatial interactions on a Fishtank virtual reality display, which is basically a stationary monitor display that can render 3D, can be used towards educating users in electrical circuitry learning using near-field spatial interaction using both hands. 

And he also compared this, for instance, to just keyboard and mouse-based interaction on a monitor display setting and also on a head-mounted display. And he found that the efficiency of movement of the dominant and non-dominant hands was actually more efficient in the head-mounted display condition, in the immersive condition, as compared to this kind of Fishtank display condition.
