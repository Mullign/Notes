There are basically certain universal interaction technique tasks that are true and common to any 3D simulation, such as video games or extended reality, virtual reality. The thing that we have spent some time talking about is travel. Travel falls under a category of tasks called navigation tasks. So travel is essentially the motor component of navigation. Wayfinding is the brain or the mind-level aspect of navigation, which is the cognitive component. So travel, again, is the motor component. How do you actually execute travel from one location to another? 

Wayfinding is the cognitive component. The cognitive component has to do with planning a route from your current location to another location. What is your intention? Which direction do you want to go? And to what extent are you able to understand the space well enough that allows you to explore it? Those are the aspects of processing that happens in the mind. And so wayfinding is this cognitive component that is associated with those processes that happen in the mind, that are associated with navigating through the space. 

Generally, the universal interaction tasks, spatial interaction tasks, have to do with navigation that consists of travel and wayfinding. And selection, you have to select objects in the scene that you want to interact with. Manipulation, which has to do with scaling the objects or taking the objects from one location, picking it, and placing it in another. So this picking and placing of objects and in the process, translating the object, rotating the object. And an example of such kind of task might be that involves selection and manipulation is picking up a coffee cup in the virtual environment from one location and moving it to another. In the process, selection and deselection is where you pick up and let go of the coffee cup. And manipulation is once it has been selected.
