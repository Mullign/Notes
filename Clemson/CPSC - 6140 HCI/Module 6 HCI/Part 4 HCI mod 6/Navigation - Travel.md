travel, as I mentioned before, is the motor component of navigation. The cognitive component of navigation is wayfinding. And travel, the motor component of navigation is essentially the movement between two locations. This involves setting the position and orientation of the user's viewpoint. And it's the most common and basic virtual environment interaction technique that is in any 3d environment that you can imagine. And it's used by almost any large scale virtual environment, where one needs to a user needs to move from one location to another, either virtually or physically. Let's take a look at this a little bit more in depth. So travel tasks are, can be categorized into three different categories, exploration, search and maneuvering. So exploration is essentially travel, which has no specific target. Then the purpose of travel here might be to build knowledge of the environment. 

So an example of this is in a virtual environment that could potentially resemble a museum, you're traveling through this virtual environment through the museum floor space without any specific target in mind. You're simply building knowledge of the elements and of the unfamiliar space of the museum and what aspects are conveyed to you through the museum. So you don't have any specific target in mind with regard to where you want to travel, no specific destination. You're just simply exploring this floor space. So that's exploration. Search can be either naive or primed. Naive travel has to do with travel to find a target whose position is not known. So you may not know exactly the target that you're looking for, the target's position that you're looking for. You may have a target in mind such as a red vase and you're simply walking through this floor space to find this red vase. So that's naive search. 

Prime search is essentially travel to a target whose position is known. You know that the red vase is actually located on the second floor of this building in the right hand corner of that floor space. So prime search is essentially traveling to the position such as the red vase in a specific corner of the second floor. You may be traveling to a target whose position is actually known to you. And in the process, when it comes to prime search, you're formulating a path of how to get from here to that specific location where you know the target's position. In the process, you build layout knowledge of the environment. Instead of just general knowledge, you have a specific layout knowledge that you do build and you move to the task location. Maneuvering has to do with some fine motion in which you travel to a position viewpoint for the task. These are oftentimes characterized by short velocity movements, very precise movements to perform a task. Let's think about catching a ball, for example, in virtual reality. So there is a gross motion phase where you may reach out with your hand to an approximate vicinity of where you expect the ball to come as you need to catch it. 

And then your fine maneuvering has to do with some small-scale fine maneuvering of your hand to adjust its position such that it would be in the correct vantage point to catch the ball. So gross motion has to do with large, high-velocity, large-scale motion. And then fine maneuvering has to do with short adjustments, very precise, low-velocity movement to adjust your position so that you can be in the best vantage point to perform the task. And that happens with any sort of motor task that we do, including ones we do in the virtual world.
