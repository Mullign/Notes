![[Gulfs of Execution and Evaluation Slides.pdf]]A related concept that was developed by Hutchins and Norman in 1986 is that the concept of gulf of execution and evaluation. These are two gulfs that they explicated that exists between the user and the interface. And these two tier theories are as follows. The gulf of execution is the gap between the user and the interface when users are interacting to provide input into the system. And the gulf of evaluation is the gulf or the gap that exists between the users and the system with regards to interpreting back. Bridging the gulfs can reduce the cognitive effort required to perform tasks is what was noted by these researchers. Here is a visual illustration of the gulf of execution and the gulf of evaluation. 

So the visual illustration actually shows that the gulf of execution actually exists between the user and the physical system. So the user may have goals, intentions, and based on these goals and intentions, they may specify an action through the interface mechanism. And so being able to cross this gulf means that one needs to implement a good interface mechanism to allow users to specify the actions based on their goals and intentions. Then the physical system actually displays, on the other hand, displays the results of the user's actions. And the evaluation bridge happens when, if the user is able to appropriately interpret what's produced by the system on the interface displays, evaluate the results that are produced by the system, and see whether it meets the goals of the user.
