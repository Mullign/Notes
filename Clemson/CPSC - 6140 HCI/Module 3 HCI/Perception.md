We have different sense organs such as eyes, ears, fingers, etc. And the information from these sense organs are transformed into experiences of objects such as depth, size, scale of objects, length, width, height, volume, speed, etc. Events like sound. Where is it coming from? What type of sound is it? How is it reverberating in the environment? 

What is the directionality of the sound? Taste. What does it taste like? What are the kinds of tastes that are engaged with this particular object? So those are the perceptual aspects that we are talking about with regards to interaction design here. And they have obvious implications to the design representations that are readily perceivable, such as text should be legible, icons should be easy to distinguish and read, and vision has been noted as the most dominant sense for sighted individuals. And for those who are not sighted, perhaps tactile sensory information such as braille is the dominant information followed by auditory information as well. And for sighted individuals, vision is the dominant information followed by hearing and touch.
