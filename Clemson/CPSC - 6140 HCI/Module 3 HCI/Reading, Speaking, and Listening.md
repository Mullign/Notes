![[Reading, Speaking, and Listening Slides.pdf]]Let's now look at readings, speaking, and listening. The ease with which people can read, listen, and speak differs. Many people prefer listening to reading. Reading can be quicker than speaking or listening. Listening requires less cognitive demand or cognitive effort than reading or speaking. Another important aspect that we need to remember when we are designing is dyslexic. Dyslexic users have difficulties understanding and recognizing written words. 

Oftentimes with such users, perhaps audio information in addition to words would be very helpful and helpful indeed in the design of computer system interfaces. A multi-modal interface might be beneficial to many users, especially in terms of promoting accessibility that incorporates audio as well as words on the screen. Applications are good applications that employ these kinds of aspects involving speaking, reading, and listening. Our speech recognition systems that allow users to interact with them by asking questions such as Google Voice, Siri, etc. Speech output systems use artificially generated speech, such as text-to-speech, and this was very helpful for users with low to no vision. And natural language systems enable users to type in questions and give text-based responses, such as the Ask search engine, for instance. Design implications for reading, speaking, and listening are as follows. 

So speech-based menus and instructions should be short. So users should be able to use very limited set of command to its speech-based menus, and the set of commands should be produced on a list so that users can be able to recognize the commands by perusing that list rather than recalling from memory. Accentuate the intonation of artificially generated speech, such as text-to-speech, because text-to-speech are harder to understand than human voices. So producing such accentuation and intonation will make it much more easier to understand. Provide opportunities for making text larger on the screen so that it's visible and salient.
