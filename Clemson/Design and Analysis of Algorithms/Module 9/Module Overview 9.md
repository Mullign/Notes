So I briefly wanted to just review some of the topics from prior modules that are highly relevant to this module that we won't be talking about in detail in this module, because we've already covered them in previous modules. So if you remember back to module one, one of the topics that we motivated when we talked about the very first programming assignment was the Nelder-Mead algorithm, or sometimes called simplex search or amoeba search, where you're basically trying to optimize a function for which, in many cases, it's hard to compute the derivatives of that function. So this is a very prominent and very popular technique for optimization, where you kind of bracket, remember, your optimal solution. If you're optimizing in two dimensions, then your bracket consists of three points. If you're optimizing in d dimensions, you need d plus one points. So basically, you're bracketing the solution with a simplex, with a generalization of a triangle. And in every iteration, you basically evaluate the objective function at those corner points that serve as the bracket around your solution. 

And you find out which one of those is the worst, and you move that in the direction of the other corner points. And so your simplex, your bracket, kind of shrinks in around as it encloses the optimal solution until eventually it kind of converges on the optimal solution. Variations of this method even allow the bracket, the simplex, to move. So you might take the worst corner point and move it so far in the direction of the other corner points that it actually kind of makes the simplex tumble over end to end. So in many variations on this algorithm, it's even possible for the simplex to kind of tumble around through space until it actually reaches a promising area, and then it starts contracting in around the optimal solution. So a good method to be aware of, especially for very complicated objectives where you can't easily find their derivatives, and you can't apply many of the other techniques that we talk about in this module, like gradient descent or Newton's method. Very closely related to the Nelder-Mead algorithm are population-based optimization methods.
