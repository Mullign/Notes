In fact, we sometimes actually formally define the so-called edge connectivity between two nodes in a graph exactly this way. So lambda of xy, we call this the edge connectivity between x and y, usually in an undirected graph in this situation. This is defined as either the maximum number of edge disjoint paths you can find between x and y, or as the minimum number of edges you have to remove from the graph to separate x from y. Those two things are equivalent. And it is usually to your advantage to have that value be large, to have a large edge connectivity between important pairs of nodes in your graph, because that means you have a lot of built-in fault tolerance, a lot of built-in redundancy. You're not going to accidentally separate x from y by breaking edges in your network unless you break a lot of edges. And so max-flows and min-cuts give you a nice way to characterize higher levels of connectivity beyond what we talked about with just depth-first search, which was, you know, just are these two nodes connected by a path?
