![[Range Queries Big Idea 2.pdf]][Music] Idea number 2 in our list of five big ideas. This one is basically, if you'd like to get a range, one way that you can get a range is by gluing together a suffix on one side and a prefix on the other side. A lot of situations where this kind of idea applies, would be, I would characterize them as you're building a two-sided data structure by gluing together two one-sided data structures. In our discussion to follow in a future video, when we talk about amortization, we're going to talk about a data structure called a min q, that we're going to obtain by gluing together two mean stacks back to back. A lot of times you can glue together two one-sided things to get a two-sided range effectively. The use case here, is a pretty useful problem. I would like to slide a window across an array, and keep track of a running minimum within that window. 

Basically output the minimum of that length-L window as it scans across the array. You can actually do that in just linear time with a nice trick. What I'm going to do is divide my array up into blocks, again, with the size of the block being equal to the size of my window. If I do that, then as I slide the window across, then the window is always going to straddle two blocks. If I look at the range from i-j, I can characterize my window as a suffix from i onwards in i's block, followed by a prefix up to j in j's block. If I have already precomputed the minimum of that suffix from i onwards and that prefix up to j, then I just take the smaller of those two numbers and that's the minimum of the entire window. The idea is I'm going to basically compute for every element in the sequence, the minimum of the suffix from that element forward within its block, and the minimum of the prefix up to that element within its block. 

I have these things already precomputed, and I can just compute the min of any window in constant time as the window scans across. How do I compute these prefix and suffix mins? That's actually quite easy. If we look at the prefix mins, I'm just going to scan across the entire array. Scan across the entire array, keep a running minimum. That's going to tell me the minimum up to each point, so write those numbers down. Every time I cross a block boundary, I just reset that running minimum. 

As I do that, that's going to tell me for every element like j, what is the minimum up to j from the most recent start of its block. Then I do the same scan scanning backwards to get the suffix minimum. For every element i, I'll then know the minimum from i forward to the next block boundary. I have this suffix and prefix that I'm gluing together, to get an answer for the overall range. This is a phenomenon that I do see quite often in the design of algorithms and data structures, piecing together information about a range from smaller components that usually represent one-sided answers. [Music]
