# **Strategy #1: The Pomodoro Technique**

### This technique has been shown to reduce the impact of distractions and interruptions and enhance productivity. By breaking work into intervals, you could improve focus, creative freshness, and concentration. True mastery of The Pomodoro Technique takes from seven to twenty days of constant use.

- Step 1: Choose a task you'd like to get done.
    
- Step 2: Set a timer for 25 minutes.
    
- Step 3: Remove potential distractions.
    
- Step 4: Focus on the task intensely until the timer runs out.
    
- Step 5: Take a short break.

# **Strategy #3: Take Effective Notes**

We recommend taking brief notes after videos and readings. Here are some best practices for taking notes:

- Keep one space for notes: Have only one space where you write and keep your notes (a notebook, a Google document, etc.)
    
- Record key points only: It doesn't matter whether your notes are digital or handwritten, just as long as you record key points instead of writing what you hear or read word-for-word. According to Robyn Scott's article "[The 30 Second Habit That Can Have a Big Impact On Your Life](http://www.huffingtonpost.com/robyn-scott/the-30-second-habit-that-_b_4808632.html "The 30 Second Habit That Can Have a Big Impact On Your Life Article")," after each lecture or reading, spend 30 seconds writing down key points and do not get boggled down in details.
    
- Take written notes while watching. Write down key ideas, not complete sentences.

# **Strategy #4: Do One Thing at a Time**

- Read Forbes' ["Multitasking Damages Your Brain And Career, New Studies Suggest"](https://www.forbes.com/sites/travisbradberry/2014/10/08/multitasking-damages-your-brain-and-career-new-studies-suggest/#4ce0a28956ee "Multitasking Damages Your Brain And Career, New Studies Suggest Article") (Travis Bradberry, October 8, 2014), which states that multitasking is less productive than doing a single thing at a time. "People who are regularly bombarded with several streams of electronic information cannot pay attention, recall information, or switch from one job to another as well as those who complete one task at a time."
