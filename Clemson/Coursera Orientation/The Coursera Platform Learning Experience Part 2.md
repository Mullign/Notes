Depending on your course, 

there are many different types of 

assignments that you will complete. 

These include quizzes, peer reviews, 

staff graded projects, programming assignments, 

and graded discussion prompts. 

Let's go through the assignment types 

for each week in the course. 

Starting with the week one quiz. 

A quiz may include a variety of 

questions for multiple choice, 

to free response, and reflective. 

Quizzes are automatically graded. 

So you will see your assignment score 

immediately after completing the quiz.

While there are quizzes which are automatically graded, 

you will also come across 

assignments that are graded by your peers. 

There are two parts to peer graded assignments. 

First, you will need to submit your own assignment, 

then you will need to review your peers. 

Remember that you will only be allowed to review 

your peers once your own assignment has been submitted. 

You can find the minimum number of peers to review here. 

Both parts will be marked as complete with 

a green check mark when you receive 

a passing grade for your own submission, 

and review the minimum number of peers.

Let's move on to graded discussion prompts, 

which is an assignment that is typically graded by 

core staff rather than your classmates. 

A graded discussion prompt is 

one assignment type you 

may need to complete in your course. 

Please note that there's a difference 

between regular discussion 

prompts like this one titled share your story, 

which is ungraded and shows up in the discussion forum, 

versus a graded discussion prompt which is not 

included in the discussion forum 

because it's a graded assignment. 

You can navigate to a graded discussion prompt from 

the grades page or by clicking the item here. 

All graded discussion prompts 

will require you to first submit 

your own response before you can 

see your peers responses. 

Your core staff will be using a rubric to 

evaluate your response and 

participation for the assignment. 

In addition to graded discussion prompts, 

you may need to complete other 

staff graded assignments like 

individual or group projects that are 

typically graded by instructors and TAs.

Depending on your program, 

you may be asked to complete 

programming assignments like this one. 

Some programming assignments are 

graded using a built-in algorithm that 

compares a learner's file submission 

to a value specified by your instructor. 

If a programming assignment uses built-in grading, 

your code will run locally on your computer, 

and the output file will be uploaded to Coursera servers. 

Your grade will be based on a comparison against 

numeric or regular expression grading logic, 

and you'll get a grade shortly after submitting. 

Others are graded using 

custom grading logic created by your instructor. 

If your programming assignment 

uses a custom grading tool, 

your code will be created either 

locally or in a Jupiter notebook.
