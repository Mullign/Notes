# We’ve filed a law­suit chal­leng­ing GitHub Copi­lot, an AI prod­uct that relies on unprece­dented open-source soft­ware piracy. Because AI needs to be fair & eth­i­cal for every­one.

## NOVEM­BER 3, 2022

Hello. This is [Matthew Butterick](https://matthewbutterick.com/ "Website for Matthew Butterick"). On Octo­ber 17 [I told you](https://githubcopilotinvestigation.com/ "GitHub Copilot Investigation Article - Maybe you don’t mind if GitHub Copi­lot used your open-source code with­out ask­ing.") that I had teamed up with the [amaz­ingly excel­lent](https://www.saverilawfirm.com/our-cases/ "Joseph Saveri Law Firm Cases & Investigations") class-action lit­i­ga­tors [Joseph Saveri](https://www.saverilawfirm.com/attorneys/joseph-r-saveri/ "Page for Joseph R. Saveri"), [Cadio Zir­poli](https://www.saverilawfirm.com/attorneys/cadio-zirpoli "Page for Cadio Zirpoli"), and Travis Man­fredi at the [Joseph Saveri Law Firm](https://www.saverilawfirm.com/our-cases/github-copilot-intellectual-property-investigation "Joseph Saveri Law Firm Case - GitHub and Copilot Intellectual Property Litigation") to inves­ti­gate GitHub Copi­lot.

Today, we’ve filed a class-action law­suit in [US fed­eral court](https://www.cand.uscourts.gov/ "Home Page, United States District Court, Northern District of California") in San Fran­cisco, CA on behalf of a pro­posed class of pos­si­bly mil­lions of GitHub users. We are chal­leng­ing the legal­ity of [GitHub Copi­lot](https://github.com/features/copilot "GitHub Copilot Home Page") (and a related prod­uct, [OpenAI Codex](https://openai.com/blog/openai-codex/ "OpenAI Blog, OpenAI Codex"), which pow­ers Copi­lot). The suit has been filed against a set of defen­dants that includes [GitHub](https://github.com/about "GitHub About Page"), [Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/about "Microsoft About Page") (owner of GitHub), and [OpenAI](https://openai.com/ "OpenAI").

By train­ing their AI sys­tems on pub­lic GitHub repos­i­to­ries (though based on their pub­lic state­ments, pos­si­bly much more) we con­tend that the defen­dants have vio­lated the legal rights of a vast num­ber of cre­ators who posted code or other work under cer­tain open-source licenses on GitHub. Which licenses? A set of 11 pop­u­lar open-source licenses that all require attri­bu­tion of the author’s name and copy­right, includ­ing the [MIT license](https://opensource.org/licenses/MIT "The MIT License - Open Source Initiative"), the [GPL](https://opensource.org/licenses/GPL-3.0 "GNU General Public License version 3 - Open Source Initiative"), and the [Apache license](https://opensource.org/licenses/Apache-2.0 "Apache License, Version 2.0 - Open Source Initiative"). (These are enu­mer­ated in the [appen­dix to the com­plaint](https://githubcopilotlitigation.com/pdf/06823/1-1-github_complaint_appendix_a.pdf "PDF of Appendix A of class-action complaint").)

In addi­tion to vio­lat­ing the attri­bu­tion require­ments of these licenses, we con­tend that the defen­dants have vio­lated:

- GitHub’s own [terms of ser­vice](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/github-terms/github-terms-of-service "GitHub Terms of Service - GitHub Docs") and [pri­vacy poli­cies](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-privacy-statement "GitHub General Privacy Statement - GitHub Docs");
    
- [DMCA § 1202](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/1202 "17 U.S. Code § 1202 - Legal Information Institute, Cornell Law School"), which for­bids the removal of copy­right-man­age­ment infor­ma­tion;
    
- the [Cal­i­for­nia Con­sumer Pri­vacy Act](https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa "California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) - State of California");
    
- and other laws giv­ing rise to related legal claims.
    

In the weeks ahead, we will likely amend this com­plaint to add other par­ties and claims.

This is the first step in what will be a long jour­ney. As far as we know, this is the first class-action case in the US chal­leng­ing the train­ing and out­put of AI sys­tems. It will not be the last. AI sys­tems are not exempt from the law. Those who cre­ate and oper­ate these sys­tems must remain account­able. If com­pa­nies like Microsoft, GitHub, and OpenAI choose to dis­re­gard the law, they should not expect that we the pub­lic will sit still. AI needs to be fair & eth­i­cal for every­one. If it’s not, then it can never achieve its vaunted aims of ele­vat­ing human­ity. It will just become another way for the priv­i­leged few to profit from the work of the many.
