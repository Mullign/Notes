AI has definitely changed the game in software development, especially with tools like GitHub Copilot and ChatGPT. Instead of spending countless hours searching online for how to complete certain tasks, these tools can quickly explain concepts or generate examples. The productivity boost is real—it lets coders spend more time actually building and finishing projects instead of getting stuck chasing down information they may or may not even use.

There have been a few projects I’ve worked on where AI would have saved me a lot of time. Recently, I’ve started using it for writing and debugging test cases, which helps me focus more on the actual logic of my code instead of getting bogged down in small mistakes. I’ve also used AI to fix syntax errors that used to take me hours of searching to track down. Having that instant feedback makes coding feel way less frustrating and way more efficient.

That said, there are some ethical concerns that can’t be ignored. One big question is where the AI is pulling its information from. If it’s trained on copyrighted or inaccurate code, developers could end up using something without realizing it. Another concern is how companies use AI to collect and store user data. For example, I met with a company recently that uses AI on their website to track where users are located and what products they search for. They store that information to target people with ads, which can be pretty risky if that data ever gets leaked or isn’t stored securely.

Looking ahead, I think AI will become way more than just an autocomplete tool. In the next five years, I expect it to handle more complex tasks so we won’t have to double- and triple-check everything it produces. I don’t think AI will fully replace developers, but I do think it will reduce the number of jobs needed for certain repetitive tasks. Instead, software developers will take on more guiding and leadership roles, focusing on architecture, decision-making, and making sure AI is being used ethically and responsibly.
