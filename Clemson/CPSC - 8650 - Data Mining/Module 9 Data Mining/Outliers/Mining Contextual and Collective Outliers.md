​As for the connective outliners, sometimes a group of data objects form a connective ​outliner if the objects as a whole deviate significantly from the entire data set, even ​though each individual object in the group may not be an outliner. ​So basically, the individual object may not be an outliner, but as a group, they are outliners. ​To detect the connective outliners, we have to examine the structure of the data set, ​that is, the relationship between the multiple objects. ​For outliner detection in temporal data, like a time series or sequence, we want to explore ​the structures formed by time, which occur in the segments of the time series or subsequences. ​To detect the connective outliners in spatial data, then we need to explore the local areas. ​So if the data is graph or network data, we have to explore the subgraphs. ​The critical difference in connective outliner detection is that the structures are often ​not explicitly defined and need to be discovered as part of the outliner detection process.
