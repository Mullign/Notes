​So outliners are not noise, okay? ​Noise is a random error or variance in the measure of the variable, and that is not interested ​in data analysis, including our outliner detection process. ​For example, if you get a big lunch one day or one more cup of coffee than usual, those ​are just the noise data, random happens. ​It will not become interesting for anybody, okay? ​Now, outliners are interesting because they are suspected of not being generated by the ​same mechanism as the rest of the data. ​So it is very important to justify why the detected outliners are generated by some other ​mechanism. ​So for example, we make various assumptions on the rest of the data, and they show that ​the detected outliners violate those assumptions significantly. 

​So the key difference between the outliner and the noise is that noise is a random error, ​but outliners are some interesting pattern, okay? ​So outliner detection is also different from the novelty detection. ​Now, for example, when you're monitoring a social media website where new content is ​incoming, novelty detection may identify new topics and new trends in a timely manner. ​So the novel topics may initially appear as outliners, but the difference is that even ​though the outliner detection and the novelty detection share some similarity in modeling ​and detection methods, the difference is that in the novelty detection, once the new topics ​are confirmed, they are usually incorporated into the model of normal behavior so that ​follow-up instances are not treated as outliners anymore.
