​In the past, most of the data mining tasks try to maximize the accuracy. ​However, fairness, interoperability, and the robustness of the system is also important. ​A recent study in VRAM maximizes the accuracy. ​Algorithm fairness is discussed recently. ​Algorithm fairness has three kinds of fairness. ​The first is group fairness. ​Group fairness includes demographic parity. 

​The idea is that you want the predictions are independent of sensitive attributes. ​So you shouldn't give a different prediction for different people. ​Equalize the odds. The conditional probability of the same prediction result given the neighbor should be equal for different sensitive attributes. ​So different people, different sensitive attributes should not have different odds. ​And then the predictive rate parity is the conditional probability of a neighbor given the prediction result should be equal for different sensitive attributes. ​Individual fairness is another fairness we want to study. 

​The idea is to preserve individual similarity. ​That is, similar individuals should be treated similarly. ​Counterfactual fairness is to try to correct the prediction of a neighbor variable that is unfairly altered by an individual sensitivity attribute. ​So that if you change the individual's sensitive attribute, you shouldn't change the prediction. ​The algorithms for fairness include pre-processing and optimization at the training and post-processing. ​In the pre-processing, you learn the representations that remove the impact of the sensitive attributes. ​You can also optimize at the training. 

​You use regularizers or constraints to achieve tradeoffs between mitigating the bias and retaining the original mining accuracy. ​You can also use the post-processing method to find a proper threshold for each group to achieve a certain type of fairness. ​Inter-probability is important for any data mining application. ​The goal is to explain the results given by any algorithm or models, which are viewed as black boxes. ​You can visualize the summary statistics of each feature as an interpretation. ​You can also explore the self-interpretable components of the models. ​For example, you weigh in the linear model the structure of the decision trees. 

​You can also identify the key data points for interpretation. ​Robustness is very important for data mining. ​The goal is to ensure the consistency between the test result and the training result with intentional added noise or unintentional noise. ​Intentional added noise is also called adversarial attack. ​There are two kinds of methods to achieve that. ​One is called the reactive method. ​The reactive method will detect adversarial examples after the model is built. 

​The proactive method makes mining models more robust to attacks so that the attacks will not be able to break in or not be able to affect the decision result.
