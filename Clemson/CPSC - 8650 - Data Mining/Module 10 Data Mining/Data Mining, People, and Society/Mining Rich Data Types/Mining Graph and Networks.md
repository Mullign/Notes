​Now, let's study how to mine the graphs and networks. ​Now, graphs and networks consist of a set of nodes and a collection of edges to model ​all the complex systems, for example, an online social platform. ​The main task of mining graphs and networks are categorization. ​So you will have several different graph types. ​You may have an attributed data type where you have a node and edge as attributes, or ​you have planned graphs where nodes and edges have no attributes. ​Then your graph may be dynamic or static. ​So if your graph topology or attribute changes, then that's the dynamic graph, and otherwise ​it's a static graph. 

​Then your graph may be homogeneous or heterogeneous. ​So for a homogeneous node, the nodes and edges are the same type, and the heterogeneous type ​nodes are not the same type. ​For example, on the left figure is the network, all the nodes are the authors. ​And on the right side, it is a heterogeneous network. ​The network nodes include the authors and the paper and the value of the publication. ​So how do you mine the graph and the network? ​So first, you can use graph modeling to study and simulate the generation mechanism. 

​The motivation here is to understand how graphs are created and formed. ​You can also use that to help protect user privacy by normalizing the social network. ​There are different graph models. ​For example, you can use Adolphe-Renyi model, where you assume certain probability distributions. ​Or you can use realistic graph generators, where you mimic one or more properties of ​the real-world graphs. ​For example, you can use a preferential attachment-based model, such as the Barabasi-Abbott model. ​Of course, you can use deep generative models to mimic the real graphs with neural networks. 

​The model-based, graph-modeling-based approach has a challenge, how do you model the heterogeneous ​network? ​For heterogeneous network mining, you have to capture the rich demanding. ​The core concept is the meta-path, which represents a sequence of relations. ​For example, in that heterogeneous network where you have an author-paper-author relationship ​that represents the co-authorship relationship, or author-publication-value relationship represents ​the public-share value, or the field of study. ​So the applications of meta-paths include the similarity search, therefore you use that ​to improve the ranking accuracy, and then you also have node classification, so you ​use the similarity matrix to infer the node labels. ​You can also do the clustering to represent different semantics, and you can use the heterogeneous ​network embedding to construct the neighborhood. ​The challenge for such a heterogeneous network is to automatically infer the structure and ​semantics from input data. 

​Another problem in mining graph and neural network is the knowledge graph mining. ​Knowledge graph is a directed heterogeneous graph to link concepts and entities through ​human-interpretable semantics, for example, DBpedia or Google Knowledge Graph. ​Currently, the construction of the knowledge graphs is label-intensive, it is primarily ​reliant on the pre-specified ontology. ​So we might want to study the extractive construction where you extract the information and provide ​a semantic neighbor. ​You can also use the language-model-based approach to construct the knowledge graph. ​You can also use the representation-learning-based approach to predict the link and provide a ​resolved entity. ​There are several challenges. 

​The first challenge is the automatic knowledge graph construction and dynamic update using ​the above method. ​Currently, another work has been done in the medical AI, conversational AI, and academic ​search engine, so there is another research in that front.
