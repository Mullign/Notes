​So, first, we will study the current trend is on the taxonomy on construction and refinement. ​What is the taxonomy? ​Taxonomy is to organize the important concept into the semantically rich structures, which ​will play an essential role at organizing massive unstructured tax data into the native ​organized structure. ​The taxonomy is multi-phases and corpus and application dependent. ​For taxonomy generation and expansion, we can use weakly or distinctly supervised methods ​for set-expansion, embedding based in hierarchical clustering, or for adding new imagined terms ​or deleting old, absent ones. ​For weakly supervised tax classification, the idea is to classify the massive diverse ​tax data in multiple granularities. ​There are three different ideas for weakly supervised tax classification. 

​The first idea is to generate the pseudodocument by embedding methods. ​Then you train the neural network based on the pseudodocument and enable the tax data. ​The second idea is to generate the cost-distinctive keywords or phrases by category-guided embedding. ​So this is the weakly supervised. ​The third idea is to use the pre-trained language model to generate the cost-distinctive keywords. ​So the pre-trained language model is kind of weakly supervised. ​For taxonomy expansion and refinement, we need fine-grain information extraction. 

​The fine-grain information extraction includes the fine-grain entity recognition. ​We want to extract the concrete role that an entity plays in a particular context. ​Therefore we have to identify the context of an entity. ​To identify the context of the entity, we have to generate the entity-mentioning candidates ​and the relation phrase. ​Then we find the most appropriate fine-grain types for the entity to be examined. ​We can use distant supervision-based methods, or embedding-based methods, or taxonomy-guided ​supervision or pre-trained language models for fine-grain information extraction. ​Knowledge graph or information network construction is another topic to be studied recently. 

​For the knowledge graph, it includes a set of entities and a set of edges linked to those entities. ​Now those edges may be labeled. ​We use distant or weak supervision for the knowledge graph. ​We use taxonomy-guided tax classification. ​There are several problems of building the knowledge graph. ​First, an entity can be associated with different attributes and values. ​It is easy to cause confusion if different associations are merged into a single knowledge graph. 

​To solve such problems, we can construct local knowledge graphs and use such local knowledge ​graphs under similar conditions.
