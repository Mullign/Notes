​Another type of study is on human-algorithm interaction. ​The goal of human-algorithm interaction is to optimize the collaboration between human ​intelligence and the algorithm, and then consequently enhance the system performance and user experience. ​Cost-sourcing is a popular approach where the goal is to harness the human intelligence ​to resolve difficult problems. ​Those problems include the label generation, model evaluation, and evaluating prediction ​interoperability, debug AI systems, and some other applications. ​Human-algorithm interaction also has another approach called human-in-the-loop, in which ​you leverage human innovation to improve the algorithm. ​For example, when you use supervised learning, you often want the human to be involved for ​double-checking the label, so you will provide a high-quality label generation for supervised ​learning. ​Or maybe you want to enhance the model's explainability with background knowledge. 

​And the model evaluation is often involved with the human. ​For human-algorithm interaction, machine-in-the-loop is another scenario where you try to improve ​the human's query strategy by the algorithm itself. ​For example, application of creative writing and joining will achieve the comparable model ​performance with reducing the demand for labeled data. ​The human-machine teaming is advanced research recently. ​The goal is to foster the effective teamwork between humans and algorithms. ​In the human-machine teaming applications, the human members are often involved in annotation ​and decision during the learning process. ​And the machine part, which is the agent members, will work as explanations and feedbacks to ​teach the humans to learn.
